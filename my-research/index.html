My Research

My research focuses on the prosocial and pro-environmental uses and effects of augmented and virtual reality technology.

Current projects

Virtual Risks and Hero Projects

  • $499,913 grant from NOAA
  • Deliverable: wearable AR Lens for Specs

NOAA fundedCo-PI2025, two yearsVR / AR + AI

Full title: Activating Transformative Community Actions for Preparedness Against Extreme Weather Events through Extended Reality and Artificial Intelligence. Led by Sun Joo (Grace) Ahn at the University of Georgia, with the University of Oregon as partner.

We use immersive technology and AI to make the risks of tropical cyclones and fire weather feel personally relevant, which can move people to prepare. The project also works with communities to co-develop plans for preparedness actions that build resilience to extreme weather.

Funded through NOAA’s Social, Behavioral, and Economic Science program in fiscal year 2025.

See the award on NOAA’s site

Ko-Kwel: He Carries a Story

  • 1st place, Spectacles Community Challenge #12 ($14,000)
  • AWE Auggie Award finalist, Best Societal Impact

Augmented realitySnap SpectaclesCoquille Indian TribeSnap AR Scholars

An augmented reality retelling of the Coquille Tribe’s Sasquatch legend. The story was shared by Jason Younker, past chief of the Coquille Indian Tribe and a University of Oregon associate vice president, and built in Snap’s Lens Studio by the Snap AR Scholars, a student-led social impact team I oversee at the School of Journalism and Communication in Portland.

Wearing AR glasses, you follow the story: hold still so Sasquatch does not see you, search for the obsidian blade, and learn his weakness from a younger Sasquatch in the lair.

It took first place in Spectacles Community Challenge #12 and was a finalist for the Auggie Award for Best Societal Impact at AWE. It runs as a location-based experience at the Oregon Reality Lab in Portland, and we are running trials to measure its impact against traditional media.
